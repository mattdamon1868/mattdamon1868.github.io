On August 18th and 19th, 2026, I attended the Actuate conference in San Francisco. The event focused on all things related to "Physical AI" or, in simpler terms, robotics. It was hosted by Foxglove.

Foxglove did a great job putting together the event. I enjoyed the talks and panel discussions about robotics and the idea of a "robots like ChatGPT" moment. A lot of the excitement is about creating robots with general intelligence that is, robots that can understand prompts from people and then perform tasks. Think about asking a robot to clean the dishes or fold laundry. While this kind of AI is important and may become common in the next 5 to 10 years, most of the engineers and companies working in this field focus on more practical uses. These are robots designed for specific tasks that are repetitive, dangerous, or helpful for caregiving, like Path Robotics or Hello Robot. These companies may not be as famous in the world of general AI, but they focus on a clear goal: solving real problems. For example, Path Robotics is working on improving welding in manufacturing. 

There might still be room for more advanced general intelligence robots someday, but it seems like a very big challenge. Developing such robots would require a huge amount of data, and there’s a lot of uncertainty about whether we’re collecting the right kind of data since a general AI needs to handle many different situations. For comparison, autonomous cars have been in development for decades, and only recently are they able to drive mostly on their own after millions of miles driven in the real world and in simulations. But creating a general AI that can handle many different situations and outcomes is even more complicated.

At this point, it feels like achieving true general intelligence in robots is a very ambitious goal. That doesn’t mean we shouldn’t research it, but instead, we should focus on advancing the "physical intelligence" that is currently solving real, tangible problems. Using this kind of AI right now can help make human lives and society more efficient.

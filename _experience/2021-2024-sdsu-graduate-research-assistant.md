At the DSIM Lab, I collaborated with a research team pursuing novel control theory approaches for autonomous systems, particularly the development of smart autonomous mobile robots.

I led the development of an adaptive control approach extremum seeking control (ESC) implemented in ROS2 for mobile robot motion. The robot has no prior knowledge of its environment, but through real-time continuous optimization it perturbs its motion toward a source (e.g., an acoustic or light signal).

This project compared prior methods against our novel design, showing improved efficiency in reaching an optimal point whose position is unknown beforehand. By actuating a sensor on a servo, the robot gained a wider search footprint, using information from the rotating sensor to locate the source of sound or light in a 25 m² test environment roughly 1000% more time and energy-efficient than prior approaches. This work became the basis for the [publication](/publications/) on servo-actuated local map exploration.
